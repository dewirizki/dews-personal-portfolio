Case 04 · Math → DE centerpiece

Datathon Kecerdasan Artifisial 2021 — PPKM policy effectiveness (EDA + clustering)

National finalist, Top 10 of 187 teams · Team lead · August 2021
Problem
Quantify whether Indonesia's PPKM social-restriction policy actually reduced workplace mobility and community transmission across Java's six provinces — evidence the government could use to decide whether to keep the policy.
Context
National AI/data competition (team "Alpaca," 3 Mathematics students); led the team and the analysis.
My role
Team lead: defined the analytical approach, ran the correlation and clustering analysis, and produced the final report.
Approach / architecture
Merged Indonesian Ministry of Health case/hospitalization/death data with Google COVID-19 Community Mobility data; ran Pearson correlation to test relationships, then K-Means clustering (with silhouette analysis to pick the cluster count) to segment provinces by transmission severity.
Technologies used
PythonPearson correlationK-Means clusteringSilhouette analysisEDA
Key implementation details
Correlation results: confirmed cases vs. workplace mobility r = −0.52; hospitalizations vs. workplace mobility r = −0.88; no meaningful correlation between deaths and workplace mobility. Selected k = 4 clusters via silhouette analysis (score 0.514), segmenting provinces into low / moderate / high / very-high transmission tiers.
Challenges
Reconciling two public datasets on different time windows and granularity (health-ministry data vs. Google mobility data), and choosing a defensible, non-arbitrary number of clusters rather than picking one by eye.
Solution
Silhouette coefficient analysis to statistically justify k = 4 instead of guessing; a correlation heatmap to validate which variables were worth clustering on before running K-Means.
Results / impact
Top 10 national finalist out of 187 teams. Found that DKI Jakarta's workplace mobility fell 52.20% versus baseline during Emergency PPKM, and that by the fourth week of PPKM Level, community transmission had dropped to low across Java provinces except D.I. Yogyakarta.
What I learned
This is the clearest through-line from Mathematics to Data Engineering: correlation and cluster validation aren't classroom exercises here, they're how you make an evidence-based case on a real policy question — the same rigor now applied to validating pipeline output rather than public health data.
